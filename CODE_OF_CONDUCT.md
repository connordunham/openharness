# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the work,
not the person doing it.

## What that means here

OpenHarness is a technical project in a fairly narrow field, and most people
who show up will know something the rest of us don't. That is the whole value
of it being open. So:

- **Disagree freely, on substance.** "This rule is wrong and here is the
  design it mis-handles" is exactly what this project needs. "You clearly
  don't know what you're doing" is not the same sentence and does not carry
  the same information.
- **Take correction well.** Being told your rule is wrong by someone who
  builds harnesses for a living is a good day, not a bad one.
- **Don't perform expertise.** Say what you know, say what you're inferring,
  and say which is which. That habit is written into the contributing guide
  for the code; it applies to conversation too.
- **No harassment, personal attacks, sustained disruption, or unwelcome
  attention.** No demeaning or exclusionary comments about anyone's identity —
  age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity, nationality, personal
  appearance, race, religion, sexual orientation, or anything else in that
  family.
- **Assume good faith about delays.** This is a small project. Silence is
  bandwidth, not contempt.

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and
anywhere someone is representing the project.

## If something goes wrong

Report it to the maintainer, privately, at the contact on
[connordunham](https://github.com/connordunham)'s GitHub profile. Include what
happened and where. Reports are handled privately and we will not share your
identity with the person reported without asking you first.

Responses range from a private word, to editing or removing content, to a
temporary or permanent ban, depending on what happened. We will tell you what
we did.

If a report concerns the maintainer, and you would rather not raise it with
him, GitHub's own [abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse)
route exists and is a legitimate thing to use.
